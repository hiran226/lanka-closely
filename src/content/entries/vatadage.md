## What it meant

A vatadage is a circular relic house. A small stupa sits at the centre on concentric stone platforms, surrounded by rings of stone pillars that once supported a roof sheltering it.

That roof is the missing piece. It was probably timber and possibly conical, but its form is debated, so every reconstruction you see on a signboard is an educated guess. The pillars are the evidence; the roof is the argument.

At Polonnaruwa the plan is unusually clear. A lower platform is entered by a single north door; the upper one has four entrances at the cardinal points, each faced by a seated Buddha around the central stupa.

## How it changed

The three best examples show the idea at different stages. At Tiriyaya, in Trincomalee district, a small stupa was enlarged in the 8th century CE and a vatadage built around it later. Medirigiriya has two or three rings of pillars and four meditating Buddhas, and is often thought the most intact and elegant. Polonnaruwa's adds the full entrance ensemble of moonstone and guardstones.

## Notice this

Whichever of Polonnaruwa's four stairs you climb, a Buddha is already facing you.
