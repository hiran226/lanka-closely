## What it meant

A stupa (dagaba, vehera or chaitya) is a solid relic monument. There is no inside to visit: the relic lies sealed in the dhatu garbha, a chamber within the dome.

Read it from the ground up. Three diminishing terraces (pesa valalu) carry the dome (garbha). Above sits a square "tee cube" (hatharas kotuwa), then a cylindrical neck with deity figures (devatha kotuwa), the conical spire (koth kerella) and the pinnacle (kotha), finished with a crystal or metal finial.

## How it changed

Builders worked with six classical dome shapes: bubble, bell, pot, heap of paddy, lotus and amalaka, the gooseberry. Jetavana, Abhayagiri, Ruwanweli and Mirisaveti were originally heaps of paddy; Ruwanwelisaya today is a bubble.

Scale escalated too. Thuparama, from the 3rd century BCE, is the oldest. Jetavanaramaya, raised under Mahasena, stood about 122 m high when complete: the tallest stupa ever built and the largest brick structure of the pre-modern world. From around the 2nd century CE, vahalkada frontispieces appeared at the four cardinal points, crowded with animals, flowers, swans and dwarfs.

## Notice this

Look at the outline before the size. The silhouette tells you which of the six shapes you are facing.
