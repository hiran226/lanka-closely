## What it meant

Line rooms are long rows of attached single-room dwellings, built for the workers who made the plantations run. Most of those workers were Malaiyaha, or Hill Country, Tamils, recruited from South India (from Tirunelveli, Tiruchi, Madurai and Thanjavur) from the 1820s onwards. In 2023 the community marked 200 years since the arrivals of 1823.

So the lines are not a building type to be collected. They are where a community has lived, raised families and kept its traditions for two centuries, with the estate kovil and the factory close by.

## How it changed

The first crop was coffee. When leaf-rust fungus destroyed it, between the late 1860s and the 1880s, estates turned to tea. James Taylor, a Scot who arrived in 1852, planted 19 acres at Loolecondera near Kandy in 1867, had a factory working by 1872, and Ceylon tea reached London by 1875.

Taylor's is the name visitors usually hear. The work of picking, carrying and processing was done by Malaiyaha Tamil families, and a museum dedicated to their history now exists.

## Notice this

On an estate road, look for the lines, the kovil and the factory together. Home, worship and work belong to one landscape, and the tea bushes are only part of it.
