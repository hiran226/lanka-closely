## What it meant

Read a moonstone from the outside in, the way your feet approach it. On the mature Anuradhapura stones the rings run: a fringe of flames, a procession of elephant, horse, lion and bull, a band of scrolling creeper (liyavel), a line of geese, and finally a half-lotus at the centre.

The best-known interpretation is Senerat Paranavitana's: flames for the suffering of samsara, the four beasts for birth, old age, disease and death, the creeper for craving, the geese for the power to tell good from bad, the lotus for nirvana. It has been hugely influential and also challenged. A plainer reading will do perfectly well on site: this is a threshold, easing you from the ordinary world into a sacred one.

## How it changed

The form may have begun as a plain square slab, and square moonstones still survive. At Polonnaruwa the carving grows busier, the animals often take separate bands and the bull usually vanishes, commonly explained as deference to the bull's place in Hindu belief under South Indian influence. Kandyan moonstones flatten and simplify, sometimes to a lotus and a border.

## Notice this

The animals are not standing still. Paranavitana described them as racing one another round the arc, and once you see the gallop you cannot unsee it.
