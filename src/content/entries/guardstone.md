## What it meant

Guardstones stand upright on either side of a stair or doorway, completing the entrance ensemble with the moonstone at your feet and the balustrades at your hands. They protect the threshold, but they also promise something. The purna ghata, a pot overflowing with growth, is a sign of abundance.

The finest show a naga-raja, the serpent king, in human form and crowned with a many-headed cobra hood. He holds the pot of abundance, often a flowering branch or staff as well, and is sometimes attended by dwarfs.

## How it changed

The archaeologist Charles Godakumbura described five phases, beginning with a wooden prototype. In stone the sequence runs from a plain slab, to the pot of abundance, to a dwarf or gana figure, and finally to the naga king. Finding a plain slab and a fully carved naga-raja in the same ancient city is a quiet lesson in how a form matures over centuries.

## Notice this

At Ratnaprasada, check which hand holds the pot. It is the left: a small detail most visitors walk straight past.
