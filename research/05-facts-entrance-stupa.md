## The entrance ensemble
Moonstone (sandakada pahana) + guardstones (muragala) + balustrades (korawak gala) = the standard three-part entrance of Buddhist buildings.

## Moonstone — sandakada pahana
- Semicircular carved slab at the foot of steps into a shrine/monastery. Read outside → in.
- Mature Anuradhapura sequence, outside in: (1) flames, (2) procession of four beasts — elephant, horse, lion, bull, (3) creeper/foliage (liyavel), (4) geese/hamsa, (5) central half-lotus. Paranavitana: "a conventional half-lotus in the centre enclosed by concentric bands…" (geese, foliage, four beasts racing each other, flames).
- Paranavitana's reading (influential, NOT settled; others have challenged it): flames = suffering of samsara; four animals = birth, decay/old age, disease, death; creeper = craving; geese = discernment between good and bad (swan separating milk from water); lotus = nirvana/purity.
- Simpler reading: a threshold guiding the worshipper from ordinary world to sacred interior.
- Possibly originated as plain square slabs; square moonstones exist (amazinglanka).
- Polonnaruwa: more elaborate, animals often in separate bands, bull commonly omitted (often explained as respect for the bull in Hindu belief under South Indian influence — present as a common explanation).
- Kandyan: flatter, stylised, sometimes just lotus + border.
- Best: Mahasena's Palace, Anuradhapura (finest, rarely visited; Abhayagiri area, c. 7th–8th c. per wanderlog — treat date cautiously); Ratnaprasada, Abhayagiri; Polonnaruwa Vatadage (later style).

## Guardstones — muragala
- Upright stones flanking stair/doorway. Development (Charles Godakumbura described 5 phases incl. wooden prototype): plain slab → purna ghata (pot of abundance) → dwarf/gana figure → naga-raja (crowned serpent-king, multi-headed cobra hood, holding purna-ghata and often a flowering branch/staff, sometimes with dwarf attendants).
- Finest: Ratnaprasada, Abhayagiri (naga-king holds purnaghata in left hand). Also Polonnaruwa Vatadage.

## Balustrades — korawak gala ("wingstones")
- Stone balustrades either side of steps. Signature ornament: the makara, its scrolling body forming the rail, open mouth at the top. Advanced makaragala at Lankatilaka.

## Makara
- Composite creature (as described for Lankatilaka): elephant trunk (dexterity), lion paws (strength), crocodile jaws (discipline), boar ears (sensitivity), fish body (movement), peacock feathers (beauty).
- Makara torana: arch with makara heads at the ends, bodies forming the curve, often a kirtimukha at the apex. Lankatilaka (Polonnaruwa) — Buddha image within makara torana; Degaldoruwa (Kandyan period) painted/carved.

## Stupa (dagoba / dagaba / vehera / chaitya)
- Solid relic monument, not enterable.
- Parts, base→top: pesa valalu (three diminishing basal terraces) → garbha (dome; relic chamber = dhatu garbha) → hatharas kotuwa (square "tee cube") → devatha kotuwa (cylindrical neck, deity figures) → koth kerella (conical spire) → kotha (pinnacle; crystal/metal finial).
- Six classical dome shapes: bubble (bubbulakara) — Ruwanwelisaya (today), Rankoth & Kiri Vehera Polonnaruwa; bell (ghantakara) — Ambasthala, Mihintale; pot (ghatakara); heap of paddy (dhanyakara) — Jetavana, Abhayagiri, Ruwanweli, Mirisaveti originally; lotus (padmakara); amalaka (gooseberry).
- Jetavanaramaya: ~122 m high when complete, diameter ~112–113 m; 3rd/4th c. (Mahasena); tallest stupa ever built, among tallest structures of the ancient world; largest brick structure of the pre-modern world.
- Abhayagiri: ~112 m ancient height. Ruwanwelisaya: ~91.5 m (Dutugemunu, 2nd c. BCE), continuously maintained.
- Thuparama: oldest, 3rd c. BCE.
- Vahalkada: ornamental frontispieces at the four cardinal points, from ~2nd c. CE; animals, flowers, swans, dwarfs.

## Vatadage
- Circular relic house built around a small stupa to shelter it; concentric stone platforms, rings of stone pillars that once held a (probably timber) conical roof — roof form is debated.
- Polonnaruwa Vatadage: two platforms; lower entered by single north door; upper has four entrances at cardinal points, central stupa, four seated Buddhas facing the entrances. Fine moonstone & guardstones.
- Medirigiriya: 2–3 rings of pillars, four meditating Buddhas; ~34 km from Polonnaruwa via Hingurakgoda. Considered the most intact/elegant.
- Tiriyaya (Thiriyaya, Trincomalee district): small stupa enlarged 8th c. CE, vatadage built later; circular platform, two colonnades.
