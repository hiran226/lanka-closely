# Research 4 — Validating "a field guide to reading Sri Lanka" (via Perplexity, 2026-11-10)

Thread: https://www.perplexity.ai/session/eb14462f-bd6f-494e-ac81-d81845f74996

**Verdict: real gap.** Existing resources are place-first ("visit Polonnaruwa"); nobody is motif-first ("here is a moonstone, how to read it, where to see 20 of them").

Existing:
- Atlas Obscura: individual curiosities, not a connected visual vocabulary.
- Lakpura / asiavela / roamingceylon / srilankaninsider: some "what to notice" sections, but tied to one site itinerary.
- exploresrilanka.lk (Renuka Jeya Raj, "Moonstone: Step into Sanctuary"): deep but text-heavy.
- Wikipedia: definitions, no traveller framing, no cross-site links.
- SLTDA Visitor Management Final Report: itself recommends multilingual signage + digital interpretation tools (i.e. official acknowledgement of the gap).

Demand evidence:
- Tripadvisor Anuradhapura: "A guide is an absolute must here, otherwise all you see is brick and stone, without understanding what you see."
- Vavuniya Univ. study (RCBS 2024, 300 foreign tourists, Anuradhapura): interpretation → satisfaction.
- Research on heritage site management: inadequate interpretation, poor signage, language barriers.
- Sentiment study (J. Sri Jayewardenepura): recommends digital guides w/ audio-visual storytelling.
- Open Univ. SL Jaffna survey: ~50% said heritage sites didn't impress at first sight.

Underserved: culture-curious independent travellers; diaspora / second-generation Sri Lankans (Sinhala and Tamil); students & teachers; design/architecture travellers.

Positioning: "Learn to see Sri Lanka" — a visual literacy guide, the companion you open standing in front of a moonstone, sluice, roofline or gopuram.
Format per detail: what you see → what it meant → how it changed → common misreadings → where to see it.
Caution: cover Tamil, Muslim, Burgher, plantation layers respectfully, not just Sinhala-Buddhist heritage.
