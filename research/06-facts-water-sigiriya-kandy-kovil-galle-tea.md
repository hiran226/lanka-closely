## Tanks (wewa) & cascades
- Wewa = earthen reservoir: long embankment (bund) across shallow valley, sluice, spillway; chain of smaller tanks downstream.
- Ellanga (ellangawa) / cascaded tank-village system (ellanga gammana): tanks linked in one micro-catchment; overflow of upper feeds lower tanks & fields. Evolved over nearly two millennia (FAO). **FAO GIAHS designation 2017** (it's FAO, not UNESCO).
- Kala Wewa (near Dambulla/Anuradhapura; Dhatusena, 5th c.): main sluice ~40 ft wide; feeds Yodha Ela / Jaya Ganga, ~87 km (54 mi) canal to Tissa Wewa, Anuradhapura; gradient ~6 inches per mile (~10 cm/km) over first 17 mi (Wikipedia: Kala Wewa, Yodha Ela; ICID World Heritage Irrigation Structures register).
- Bisokotuwa: stone-lined valve-pit (cistern) sluice regulating outflow, reducing water pressure. Avoid an exact invention date; "by the early centuries CE" / ancient period. Kala Wewa's bisokotuwa feeds Yodha Ela through its sorowwa.
- Parakrama Samudra (Polonnaruwa, Parakramabahu I, 12th c.), Tissa Wewa (Anuradhapura), Minneriya (Mahasena, 3rd c.).
- "Let not even a drop of rain water go to the sea without benefiting man" — traditionally attributed to Parakramabahu I (Culavamsa sentiment), a paraphrase, not verbatim. Present as such.

## Sigiriya
- ~180–200 m granite/magma plug, Matale district. King Kashyapa I (Kassapa), reign 477–495 CE (CCF). UNESCO 1982. Culavamsa: Kassapa fled to Sihagiri, walled it, built a lion staircase.
- Frescoes in a sheltered rock pocket, western face: female figures ("apsaras"/celestial maidens; alt. readings: court ladies, devotees bearing flowers in procession). ~22 survive (often cited, ~500 originally claimed — be cautious, say "a fraction of what was there").
- Mirror Wall: brick masonry with highly polished white plaster. Visitors from ~8th–10th c. scratched verses. Paranavitana deciphered/published 685 verses (Sigiri Graffiti, 1956); total estimated 1,500–1,800+. Among the oldest Sinhala poetry. Don't touch.
- Lion Gate: two colossal paws of brick & plaster at final ascent — remnant of a giant lion whose mouth you once walked through.
- Water gardens: symmetrical; gravity-fed fountains still work in rainy season.

## Kandyan architecture
- Steep (often two-tier) hipped roofs with small flat clay tiles (pethi ulu); timber pillars & brackets; stone plinths. Suits heavy rain.
- Temple of the Tooth (Sri Dalada Maligawa): octagonal Pattirippuwa added by Sri Vikrama Rajasinha (1798–1815), last Kandyan king, originally a royal audience pavilion. Kirti Sri Rajasinha (1747–82) expanded the temple.
- Embekke Devale: famed wood carvings on pillars & brackets (look up).
- Tampita viharaya: small timber shrine raised on stone pillars (protects from damp/termites).
- Ambalama: open-sided wayside rest pavilion for travellers.
- Misreading: not one uniform style.
- Also: Gadaladeniya (Sinhala + Dravidian mix, 14th c.), Lankatilaka (Udunuwara).

## Hindu kovil & gopuram
- Gopuram: monumental tiered gateway tower of Dravidian temples; stacked sculptural registers of painted deities; marks threshold street → sacred precinct.
- Nallur Kandaswamy Kovil, Jaffna: dedicated to Murugan (vel = spear, peacock). Present (4th) temple built 1734 by Don Juan Ragunatha Maapaana Mudaliyar during Dutch era (Tamil Wikipedia gives 1749). Nine-storey Shanmuha Raja Gopuram unveiled 21 Aug 2011 (Swarna Vaasal golden entrance); another raja gopuram 2015. Misreading: bright tower ≠ ancient.
- Nallur festival: ~25 days, Tamil month Aavani (~August), chariot (ther) procession.
- Koneswaram, Trincomalee: Shiva temple on Swami Rock headland.
- Colours identify deities & narrate Puranic stories.

## Galle Fort
- UNESCO 1988 ("Old Town of Galle and its Fortifications"). Portuguese origins (1588 per Atlas Obscura), Dutch massively rebuilt (from 1640s), then British.
- Old Gate completed by Dutch 1669: VOC relief — rooster on a rock above a shield with 'VOC', two lions, inscription ANNO MDCLXIX. British added their coat of arms on the outer face; VOC emblem is on the inner face.
- Bastions: Flag Rock, Triton, Aurora, Star, Sun, Moon. Walk ramparts at sunset.
- Dutch Reformed Church (Groote Kerk): floor gravestones moved from old Dutch cemetery — coats of arms, skulls, hourglasses.
- Verandah houses (private — view from street).

## Tea landscape
- Line rooms: long rows of attached single-room dwellings for plantation workers.
- Malaiyaha (Hill Country) Tamils: recruited from South India (Tirunelveli, Tiruchi, Madurai, Thanjavur) from 1820s (2023 marked 200 years since 1823 arrivals); first for coffee; coffee wiped out by leaf-rust fungus (late 1860s–1880s) → tea.
- James Taylor, Scottish, arrived 1852; 1867 planted 19 acres at Loolecondera (Loolkandura) near Kandy; factory by 1872; Ceylon tea in London by 1875. Buried at Mahaiyawa Cemetery, Kandy.
- Estate kovils, factories (withering lofts). Line rooms are homes — visit only with permission/community tour. A museum for Malaiyaha Tamils exists (Caravan magazine).
